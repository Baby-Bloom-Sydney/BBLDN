# 20 — Offer Architecture (Hormozi $100M Offers, through the aura)

> Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers* framework applied to Baby Bloom — **through** the brand discipline in [`WHY/04_THE_BRAND.md`](../../../WHY/04_THE_BRAND.md), not on top of it. The underlying psychology Hormozi systematises is real and useful; many of his surface tactics (manufactured urgency, scarcity theatre, exaggerated stacking, hyperbolic guarantees) are explicit "never do" patterns for this brand. This doc separates the *principle* from the *tactic* and shows which translations land.

---

## The framework Hormozi is actually offering

Strip Hormozi back to first principles and what he is teaching is **value perception engineering**. His *Value Equation*:

```
Value  =  (Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
          ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
          (Time Delay  ×  Effort & Sacrifice)
```

Increase the numerator. Decrease the denominator. The closer that ratio gets to "you'd feel stupid saying no", the closer you are to a Grand Slam Offer.

This is sound. It also maps directly onto Kahneman's prospect-theory work — Dream Outcome is the emotional weight of the gain; Perceived Likelihood is the probability weighting; Time Delay is hyperbolic discounting; Effort & Sacrifice is the cost of action. Hormozi is operationalising Kahneman without naming him.

Where Hormozi's tactical layer goes wrong for a brand like Baby Bloom: he tends to maximise via **theatre** — bonus stacking that looks like a late-night infomercial, urgency that breaks the moment users compare notes, guarantees so loud they raise suspicion. Those are out of brand. We use the equation, not the theatre.

---

## The Baby Bloom Value Equation, term by term

### Dream Outcome — what the parent is really buying

In Hormozi terms: what is the absolute best version of what they want, stated in concrete terms?

For the parent, the dream outcome is **not** "subscribe to a child development app". It is:

- *My child receives world-class, individualised, evidence-based developmental support during the years that matter most.*
- *I have a daily, visible record that my decision was right.*
- *My nanny is structured, professional, valuable — and my relationship with her is supported by a framework, not negotiated week to week.*
- *I have something most parents do not, and my child has a head start most children will never get.*
- *At the end of three years, I will have a record of who my child became.*

Hormozi's prescription: name the dream outcome clearly and put it at the top of the offer.

Baby Bloom's adaptation: **never** state these outcomes explicitly to the parent. They violate the brand's "never make promises about outcomes" rule. Instead, *show* the world the dream outcome already lives in — the radar moving, the milestone hitting, the nanny working, the photo from the park. The dream is in the parent's head; the surface evokes it. She draws her own conclusion.

This is the difference between Hormozi-style ("Your child will be in the top 1% of developmental milestones — guaranteed!") and Baby Bloom-style (a feed showing what the nanny did with the child today, a radar showing one domain ticking up). Same dream, different register.

### Perceived Likelihood of Achievement — why she believes it

Hormozi's lever: testimonials, case studies, money-back guarantees, "X people achieved Y" social proof, trial structures, "we've helped 10,000 customers" specificity.

Baby Bloom's translation:

| Hormozi tactic | Baby Bloom version |
|---|---|
| Testimonials | **Honest** parent testimonials only — when we get them. No manufactured social proof. No stock-photo families. No theatrical "Sarah from Bondi says…" graphics. The aura principle requires that any testimonial be real, named (with permission), and *true*. |
| Money-back guarantee | ACL-compliant refund policy via Contact Us. Real refunds, real reasons, real alternatives on denial. Not theatricalised — just done. |
| "X customers" social proof | Only when X is real and material. Not "1,000 happy parents!" if we've served 50. The real social proof at Baby Bloom is **the nanny's recommendation in person**, which is far more credible than any number. |
| Trial structure | **The big one.** The 30-day trial — no card required — is Baby Bloom's primary perceived-likelihood lever. The parent does not have to believe the outcome on faith; she sees it for herself, in her own home, with her own child, before paying anything. |
| Specificity | Specific milestones. Specific observations. Specific dates. The parent's perceived likelihood is engineered through the **specificity of evidence in front of her**, not through claims. |

The trial is the most powerful perceived-likelihood lever any business can offer: lived experience. Hormozi acknowledges this; Baby Bloom centres it.

### Time Delay — how fast she gets value

Hormozi's lever: minimise time from purchase to first result. "First win in 5 minutes". "Setup in under a day".

Baby Bloom's translation:

| Time-to-value moment | Time delay |
|---|---|
| Trial activation | Immediate (parent claims invite → app opens) |
| First value | Immediate — the nanny has been using the app for days/weeks before the parent joins. Parent walks into a **rich feed**, not an empty app. |
| First Katie interaction | Within minutes of opening the app. Katie's empty-state message is the first sentence of the relationship. |
| First visible milestone movement | Often within the first week as the nanny continues logging observations and completing activities. |
| First Progress radar shift | Often within the first 2 weeks. |
| First "this is paying off" emotional moment | Designed for the first 7-14 days of the trial. |

The structural decision to make the **nanny pre-populate the record before the parent joins** is exactly Hormozi's "minimise time delay" principle, applied at the architecture level. The parent does not wait for value; the value is already there when she arrives.

### Effort & Sacrifice — how hard she has to work for it

Hormozi's lever: do the work for them. Done-for-you. Pre-set defaults. Concierge onboarding. Zero learning curve.

Baby Bloom's translation:

| Effort moment | What we did to reduce it |
|---|---|
| Sign up | Single form, email + password. Welcome email immediate. |
| Connect to nanny | Single tap on invite link. No multi-step verification flow. |
| Use the app | The nanny does the heavy lifting of contribution; the parent's effort is "open and look". She can contribute too — same FAB, same Katie — but it is not required. |
| Make decisions about activities | Katie plans them. Parent does not have to invent. |
| Make decisions about observations | Katie surfaces patterns. Parent does not have to interpret. |
| Convert at trial-end | 30 seconds: pick plan, enter card, done. Same as any modern checkout. |
| Cancel | Available via Customer Portal or in-app — no FTC-targeted "click to cancel" gauntlet. |

The parent's primary commitment is *attention*, not *work*. This is the right structure for a busy professional first-time mother.

## The Grand Slam Offer for Baby Bloom

If we wrote the offer in Hormozi's voice (and then deliberately strip it down to the aura register), it would look like this:

### Hormozi voice (do NOT ship)

> 🚨 **THE BABY BLOOM EARLY DEVELOPMENT SYSTEM** 🚨
>
> For ambitious parents who refuse to settle:
>
> ✅ 30-day full access — NO CARD REQUIRED
> ✅ AI-individualised activities tailored to YOUR child's exact developmental stage
> ✅ Real-time progress tracking across 7 EYLF domains
> ✅ A dedicated nanny doing structured developmental work daily
> ✅ Katie — your AI developmental coach — included
> ✅ Daily notifications + weekly summaries
> ✅ A complete record of your child's first 3 years
> ✅ ACL-protected refund guarantee
> ✅ NO long-term commitment — cancel anytime
>
> Pay $200/month or $2,000 ONCE and you're done until age 3.
>
> **OVER 10,000 SYDNEY PARENTS CAN'T BE WRONG** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

This works for Hormozi's audience. It does not work for Baby Bloom. It violates: never speak more than necessary, never pushy, never use statistics designed to trigger inadequacy, never make outcome promises, never gamify (the emojis), never use "10,000 parents" social proof at our actual scale, never over-explain, never reveal the machinery.

### Baby Bloom voice (the version we actually ship)

> Choose how you support {{child_first_name}}'s development.
>
> **Pay once. Done until {{child_first_name}} is 3.** — A$2,000.
> *At {{child_first_name}}'s current age, that works out to about A${{effective_per_month}}/month.*
>
> **Monthly — pay as you go.** — A$200/month.
> *Cancel anytime.*
>
> A$100 of every monthly subscription supports {{nanny_first_name}}'s work with {{child_first_name}}. The upfront option includes A$1,000 toward {{nanny_first_name}}'s initial commission.

(Source: [`PAYMENTS/COPY-AND-FRAMING.md`](../PAYMENTS/COPY-AND-FRAMING.md) §1.)

The Baby Bloom version has done the Hormozi work *internally* — the upfront option is the high anchor + mathematically the better deal for younger children + the recommended option; the monthly option is the flexible fallback; the redistribution to nanny is named (real reciprocity); cancel-anytime is mentioned (effort/sacrifice minimisation). But the *register* is restraint. No emojis. No bullets piled high. No outcome claims. No social proof theatre.

Same offer architecture. Different surface. **The Hormozi-applied version converts; the aura-applied version converts AND builds the brand.**

## Bonus stacking — what counts as a bonus for Baby Bloom

Hormozi's prescription: stack bonuses to perceived multiples of the price. Each bonus addresses a specific objection.

Baby Bloom's natural bonuses — the things included that the parent might not realise are included:

| "Bonus" | What it addresses | How it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Katie included | "Will I have to figure this out myself?" | She is the front-door interface. No upsell. |
| AI activity generation | "How do I know the activities are age-appropriate?" | Every activity uses the child's exact age + targeted milestones. |
| The Progress radar | "How will I know if it's working?" | Visible movement in real time. |
| Photo + video record | "Will I have memories of this period?" | Every observation + activity report supports media. |
| Daily presence-while-absent | "Can I trust what's happening when I'm at work?" | Notifications + feed updates throughout the day. |
| Nanny commission redistribution | "Where does my money actually go?" | Half supports the nanny directly. Named in the receipt. |
| The relationship structure | "How do I keep my nanny long-term?" | The platform is the framework — her tools, her record, her professional polish. |
| ACL-compliant refund | "What if it doesn't work for us?" | Real refunds via Contact Us. |
| At-3 record continuity | "What happens at the end?" | The record is preserved. (The print-deliverable is on the deferred roadmap — Amendment 5.) |

In Hormozi's surface tactic, these would be listed as separate "bonuses worth $X each — TOTAL VALUE $4,500 YOURS FREE". In Baby Bloom's register, they are simply *what's included*, named where relevant (the receipt, the FAQ, the in-app context), never theatricalised.

The Hormozi insight to retain: **make every value layer visible**. A parent who doesn't realise the redistribution is named, or who doesn't realise Katie is included, is undervaluing the offer. Visibility is the lever. Theatre is not.

## Scarcity, urgency, guarantees — three explicit rejections

Hormozi treats these as core tools. Baby Bloom rejects two of the three.

### Scarcity — REJECTED (manufactured form)

We do not say "only 3 trial slots this week". We do not cap signups artificially. We do not run "limited edition" cohorts.

The **only** scarcity we acknowledge is the one that is real: **the developmental window**. A child is only 18 months old once. That's a fact, not a tactic. We can state it once, factually — *"the years that matter most"* — and leave it. We do not weaponise it. (See [`30-presentation-principles.md`](./30-presentation-principles.md) §The honesty register.)

### Urgency — REJECTED (manufactured form)

No countdown timers. No "subscribe now to lock in this rate". No "your trial expires at midnight" with red bold text.

The **only** urgency we honour is the one the parent feels herself: she is at trial day 27 and she has been seeing the value. The trial-end nudge sequence (T-7, T-3, T-1, T-0) is honest naming of what stops — not manufactured urgency. Her urgency is what we let her feel. (See [`PAYMENTS/COPY-AND-FRAMING.md`](../PAYMENTS/COPY-AND-FRAMING.md) §3.)

### Guarantees — ACCEPTED (in honest form)

ACL-compliant refunds. Real reasons on denial, real alternatives. The 30-day trial *is itself* the strongest guarantee available — she pays nothing until she's seen what it does.

We do not write the guarantee in big red type. We just *deliver* it.

## Pricing structure — what Hormozi would say

Hormozi's pricing instinct: **anchor high, then present the offer that feels like 10x value at half the anchor**.

Baby Bloom does this naturally:

- Upfront A$2,000 is the high anchor. It is also presented FIRST on the pricing page.
- Monthly A$200 is presented second. It is the lower-friction option for the uncertain buyer; it is also the **more expensive option over 30 months** (A$200 × 30 = A$6,000 vs A$2,000 upfront).
- The math is shown honestly: at the child's current age, this works out to about A${{effective_per_month}}/month for upfront.

The parent does the math. The conclusion she reaches is her own. We do not push.

**What we do not do:** introduce a third "decoy" option (à la Ariely / Hormozi). The Economist three-tier classic — "print + web $125, web only $59, print only $125" — would be theatre here. Two clean options is the right number. Adding a third just to manipulate would activate the parent's manipulation-detection radar and break the trust she's been building. (See [`30-presentation-principles.md`](./30-presentation-principles.md) §The honesty register.)

## What Hormozi gets right that Baby Bloom must keep applying

Strip the surface tactics and these are the principles to retain:

1. **The value equation is real.** Increase numerator (dream + likelihood), decrease denominator (time + effort). Every product decision can be checked against this.
2. **Specificity > generality.** Hormozi insists on concrete promises and concrete bonuses. Baby Bloom does this through concrete *evidence* — milestones, dates, photos, observations.
3. **The customer must perceive every layer of value.** Hormozi lists bonuses; we make every layer named-where-it-counts (receipt, in-app context).
4. **First-impression weight is massive.** Hormozi engineers the offer's opening; Baby Bloom engineers the parent's first sign-in (rich feed already populated by the nanny).
5. **Time-to-value beats marketing.** Hormozi optimises the offer; Baby Bloom optimises the actual product experience to deliver value in the first session.
6. **Make the offer easy to act on.** Hormozi removes friction; Baby Bloom removes friction at sign-up + trial-entry + cancellation alike (the symmetry is the brand point).
7. **Stack value, don't just lower price.** Hormozi never recommends competing on price alone. Baby Bloom never discounts to chase conversion; we add or remove value, not dollars.

---

## What Hormozi gets wrong for Baby Bloom

Hormozi's writing is calibrated for businesses that need to **shout to be heard** — small businesses competing for attention in saturated markets, online services with low brand equity, info-product launches where social proof is the entire moat.

Baby Bloom is the opposite category. The brand asset is *quiet*. The aura is the moat. Shouting damages the asset.

Specifically:

| Hormozi tactic | Why Baby Bloom rejects it |
|---|---|
| Loud bonus stacking | Violates "never speak more than necessary" + "never reveal the machinery". |
| Manufactured scarcity / urgency | Violates "never pushy / never pully". Also explicitly regulated under ACL / FTC. |
| Outcome guarantees | Violates "never make promises about outcomes". |
| "X customers can't be wrong" | Violates "never use statistics to trigger inadequacy"; also we don't have the customer base to make the claim real. |
| Emoji-heavy / countdown-heavy landing pages | Violates "never be cutesy" + "never be needy". |
| "Risk reversal" theatre | The trial IS the risk reversal. Theatre on top of it would dilute it. |

The structural insight is that Hormozi's framework is morally neutral but the surface tactics are pre-emptive theatricality. Baby Bloom uses the framework and skips the theatre. We engineer value perception through *delivered substance* + *honest naming*, not through marketing volume.

## The Hormozi questions every offer must answer

For any future offer Baby Bloom designs (a new tier, a partnership, a Phase 2 nursery package), the questions hold:

1. **What's the dream outcome?** (Specific. Named. Theirs, not ours.)
2. **What's the perceived likelihood that they'll achieve it?** (How will they believe — trial, evidence, lived experience?)
3. **How fast will they see first value?** (Minimise.)
4. **How much effort/sacrifice will it cost them?** (Minimise.)
5. **What's in the offer that they might not realise?** (Make visible without theatricalising.)
6. **What's the anchor price?** (Honest. Defensible. Not manipulative.)
7. **Are we shouting?** (If yes, redesign.)
8. **Would a sophisticated buyer feel respected by this?** (If no, redesign.)

Pass all eight and you have a Baby Bloom-style Grand Slam Offer. Fail any of the last two and you've slipped out of brand.
